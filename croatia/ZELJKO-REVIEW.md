## The siege · 1991–92

> From October 1991 to May 1992 Dubrovnik was cut off by land and sea and shelled by the Yugoslav People’s Army (JNA), the Montenegrin Territorial Defence and volunteers from Montenegro and eastern Herzegovina. Its defenders, few and poorly armed at first, never let the city fall. Here is the siege in dates, and the places on your trip where it happened.

- [ ] **17 Sep 1991 — The blockade**
  On 17 September 1991 the Yugoslav navy declared a blockade of Croatia’s Adriatic ports, Dubrovnik among them. When the general attack began on 1 October, its Boka command told Dubrovnik’s crisis staff that no ship might sail past its blockade line; for months, ships bound for the city were stopped and searched.
  - Anniversary 09-17 1991: The Yugoslav navy declared a blockade of Croatia’s ports, Dubrovnik among them

- [ ] **19 Sep 1991 — The commander**
  Nojko Marinović, a JNA officer commanding the barracks at Trebinje, crossed to the Croatian side on 19 September 1991, bringing the army’s plans for the attack on Dubrovnik. He led the city’s defence with a few hundred poorly armed police, Croatian National Guard and local volunteers. He was badly wounded by a landmine in March 1992, and died in 2021.
  - Anniversary 09-19 1991: Nojko Marinović, who would lead the defence of Dubrovnik, crossed to the Croatian side

- [ ] **22 Sep 1991 — The first shells**
  The war reached the Dubrovnik area on 22 September 1991, when mortar fire hit Đurinići and Vitaljina in eastern Konavle. The next day artillery struck Vitaljina and Brgat, and the first villagers were evacuated.
  - Anniversary 09-22 1991: The first shells fell on Đurinići and Vitaljina in Konavle

- [ ] **23 Sep 1991 — The Armed Boats Squadron**
  Local volunteers with fast boats formed the Armed Boats Squadron (Odred naoružanih brodova). Its first boat, the Sveti Vlaho, sailed on 23 September 1991, and through the siege its crews ran the blockade to keep a supply line open by sea.

- [ ] **1 Oct 1991 — The attack begins**
  At dawn on 1 October 1991 the JNA, the Montenegrin Territorial Defence and volunteers from Montenegro and eastern Herzegovina attacked from land, sea and air; the Museum of the Homeland War puts the attacking force at 11,000 to 13,000 men. Jets hit the transmitter on Srđ, and the city lost electricity and water when the Komolac substation was destroyed. The attackers expected Dubrovnik to fall within days.
  - Anniversary 10-01 1991: The attack on Dubrovnik began

- [ ] **Oct 1991 – 1992 — Morinj**
  From 3 October 1991 the JNA held prisoners from the Dubrovnik area, most of them from Konavle, in a camp at Morinj on the Bay of Kotor in Montenegro. Around 300 people passed through it before it closed in 1992; former prisoners told of constant beatings. A Montenegrin court later convicted four former reservists.
  - Sources: [Morinj camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morinj_camp) — Wikipedia & press; [Radio Free Europe](https://www.slobodnaevropa.org/a/morinj-logor-crna-gora/28884396.html) — Wikipedia & press; [Vreme](https://vreme.com/vreme/lica-u-vlasnistvu-jna/) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **4–7 Oct 1991 — Slano and Čilipi**
  Slano, in the Dubrovnik Littoral, fell on 4–5 October 1991. Between 5 and 7 October army units burned Čilipi (an indictment lists 90 family houses destroyed), and the airport you flew into was looted, its equipment partly carried off to Montenegro.
  - Sources: [Dubrovačko primorje: Povijest](https://www.dubrovackoprimorje.hr/povijest) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [tportal (indictment)](https://www.tportal.hr/vijesti/clanak/palio-cilipe-stize-ga-pravda-20121206) — Wikipedia & press; [Vreme: equipment taken from the airport](https://vreme.com/vesti/nadjena-oprema-oteta-tokom-opsade-dubrovnika/) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **15 Oct 1991 — Cavtat falls**
  The army took Cavtat on 15 October 1991, and Župa dubrovačka in the days after 22 October. By the end of November it held almost all the land between Pelješac and Prevlaka except Dubrovnik itself.
  - Sources: [HRT Dani ponosa: Cavtat](https://daniponosa.hrt.hr/dani-ponosa/158/15-listopada-1991-okupacija-i-oslobodenje-cavtata) — Wikipedia & press; [Domovinski rat: the occupation of Župa](https://domovinskirat.hr/2025/10/24/bila-je-to-noc-u-kojoj-se-postavljalo-zastrasujce-pitanje-je-li-dubrovnik-pao-okupacija-zupe-dubrovacke/) — Wikipedia & press; [Siege of Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Dubrovnik) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **31 Oct 1991 — The Libertas convoy**
  Led by the ferry Slavija, which had left Rijeka on 28 October, a convoy of civilian boats sailed through the blockade with aid, with Stjepan Mesić, then President of the Yugoslav Presidency, aboard. It reached Dubrovnik on 31 October. On 14 November the Slavija left the port of Gruž for Rijeka with almost 4,000 people, most of them elderly people, women and children.
  - Anniversary 10-31 1991: The Libertas convoy reached Dubrovnik through the blockade
  - Anniversary 11-14 1991: The ferry Slavija carried almost 4,000 people out of Gruž

- [ ] **Nov 1991 — The ring closes**
  Bosanka, on the plateau behind Srđ, fell on 9 November 1991 after hard fighting in which several defenders were killed. In late November, after Osojnik, Mokošica and the villages of Rijeka dubrovačka were occupied. Shells fell on Gruž, Lapad and Babin Kuk, including hotels full of refugees.
  - Anniversary 11-09 1991: Bosanka, on the plateau behind Srđ, fell after hard fighting

- [ ] **1991–1992 — A city of refugees**
  Thousands of people driven from Konavle, Župa and the Littoral were sheltered in Dubrovnik’s hotels, from Lapad and Babin Kuk, where you stayed, to the Imperial by the Pile Gate, whose top floor burned in the shelling. The city lived without mains electricity or water.

- [ ] **6 Dec 1991 — The darkest day**
  From before dawn the army shelled the city for more than ten hours, and attacked Srđ with infantry and tanks from Bosanka and Strinčjera. A few dozen defenders held Fort Imperial, with tanks right up at the fort; had it fallen, the city lay open below. In the Old Town fires gutted palaces, among them the one on Od Sigurate that housed the Dubrovnik Summer Festival, and the UN tribunal later found that the attack answered no Croatian military position. The UN Commission of Experts counted thirteen civilians killed in the Dubrovnik area that day, the most of any day of the siege, and defenders were killed too. In the Old Town the tribunal’s judgement names two of the dead: Tonči Skočko, and the photographer Pavo Urban, 23, killed while photographing the shelling; a plaque under the city bell tower marks where Pavo fell. 6 December is Dubrovnik Defenders’ Day.
  - Anniversary 12-06 1991: The worst attack on Dubrovnik; Fort Imperial held. Dubrovnik Defenders’ Day

- [ ] **26 May 1992 — The siege broken**
  As the JNA began to pull back, Dubrovnik’s defenders, by now the 163rd Brigade, broke out of the city on 26 May 1992, freeing Rijeka dubrovačka, Župa, Brgat, Orašac and Osojnik, and joined the 1st Guards Brigade fighting its way down the coast from the west. The shelling did not stop at once: the city was hit again on 29 May. By the end of June the Dubrovnik Littoral was free.

- [ ] **Oct 1992 — Konavle freed**
  Under the Tuđman–Ćosić agreement, signed in Geneva on 30 September 1992, the Yugoslav army left Konavle; its last soldiers left Prevlaka on the evening of 20 October. That night Operation Konavle began with a landing at Cavtat, and by 24 October Croatian forces stood on the borders. Prevlaka was placed under UN supervision.
  - Anniversary 10-21 1992: Operation Konavle: the last occupied part of the Dubrovnik area was freed

- [ ] **1991–1998 — The damage and the repair**
  By the city’s count about 2,000 shells and rockets struck the Old Town, and the walls took more than a hundred direct hits. A survey made with UNESCO, cited by the Hague tribunal, found that 563 of the Old Town’s 824 buildings, 68 per cent, were hit in 1991–92. Restoration followed, and in 1998 UNESCO took the Old City off its In Danger list. A map of the hits stands near the Pile Gate.
  - Sources: [City of Dubrovnik: destruction in the Old City](https://www.dubrovnik.hr/stradanje-grada/the-destruction-and-restoration-dubrovnik-1991-2000/destruction-old-city) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [UNESCO: Dubrovnik removed from the Danger List](https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/147) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [HMdb: the map of the damage](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=126979) — Travel guides & blogs; [ICTY: Strugar et al., amended indictment](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/strugar/ind/en/str-ai030331e.htm) — Scholarly & archival

- [ ] **2004–2008 — Judgement at The Hague**
  At the UN tribunal for the former Yugoslavia, Admiral Miodrag Jokić pleaded guilty over the attack of 6 December and was sentenced to seven years in 2004. General Pavle Strugar was convicted in 2005 for failing to stop it and sentenced to eight years, reduced on appeal in 2008 to seven and a half.
  - Sources: [ICTY: Strugar judgement](https://www.icty.org/en/press/judgement-case-prosecutor-v-pavle-strugar-pavle-strugar-sentenced-eight-years%E2%80%99-imprisonment) — Scholarly & archival; [ICTY: Strugar appeal judgement](https://www.icty.org/en/sid/9948) — Scholarly & archival; [Miodrag Jokić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miodrag_Joki%C4%87) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **In memory — Remembering**
  Croatian records count 182 defenders from Dubrovnik’s own units, and some 235 from other Croatian units, killed in the defence and liberation of the Dubrovnik area. The UN Commission of Experts counted at least 82, and possibly 88, civilians of the Dubrovnik area killed between September 1991 and the end of 1992. The photographs of Dubrovnik’s fallen defenders hang in the Memorial Room in the Sponza Palace; Fort Imperial houses the Museum of the Homeland War. The cross on Srđ, pulled down in 1991, was raised again in October 1997, and the cable car, wrecked in 1991, has run again since July 2010.

## Ragusa · Dubrovnik

> A refugee town on a rock became a merchant republic that kept its freedom for more than four centuries between Venice, Hungary and the Ottomans — under one word: LIBERTAS.

- [ ] **c. 614 — Founded by refugees from Epidaurum** _(tradition)_
  Emperor Constantine VII's De Administrando Imperio (c. 950) says people fleeing the Avar and Slav destruction of Epidaurum (Cavtat) settled the rock of Laus, the start of Ragusa. Digs under the cathedral have found older churches, so how old the settlement really is is still argued.
  - Sources: [Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrovnik) — Wikipedia & press; [Epidaurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidaurum) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovačka Republika](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovacka-republika) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Britannica: Dubrovnik](https://www.britannica.com/place/Dubrovnik) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **866–867 — The Saracen siege**
  Arab raiders besieged the town for about fifteen months. It held until a Byzantine fleet of some 100 ships under Niketas Ooryphas came to its relief, and Byzantium then reorganised the coast as the Theme of Dalmatia.
  - Sources: [Siege of Ragusa (866–868)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ragusa_(866%E2%80%93868)) — Wikipedia & press; [Niketas Ooryphas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niketas_Ooryphas) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovnik](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovnik) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Saraceni](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/saraceni) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **972 — St. Blaise becomes the city's patron** _(tradition)_
  Tradition dates the Festivity of St. Blaise, held every 3 February, to 972; UNESCO's record traces it with certainty to 1190 and listed it as intangible heritage in 2009.
  - Anniversary 02-03 null: The Festivity of St. Blaise, Dubrovnik's patron
  - Sources: [UNESCO: Festivity of Saint Blaise](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/00232) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1023 — Lokrum's Benedictines**
  Benedictine monks are first recorded on Lokrum, the island off the city, in 1023. From 1859 it belonged to Archduke Maximilian of Austria, later Emperor of Mexico, who laid out its gardens.
  - Sources: [Lokrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokrum) — Wikipedia & press; [Lokrum: cultural heritage](https://www.lokrum.hr/en/cultural-heritage) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1205–1358 — Under Venice**
  After the Fourth Crusade Venice took sovereignty and chose the city’s count and archbishop, but Ragusa kept its own councils, laws and trade. Resentment boiled over in revolts in 1231, 1235 and 1251; Venetian rule ended in 1358.
  - Sources: [Republic of Ragusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ragusa) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovačka Republika](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovacka-republika) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Proleksis: Dubrovačka Republika](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/18640/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1252 — Lastovo joins**
  The island of Lastovo placed itself under Ragusa of its own accord — by 1272 at the latest; the Dubrovnik diocese dates it to 1252.
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovačka Republika](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovacka-republika) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Dubrovačka biskupija: Sveti Vlaho](https://db.hr/sveti-vlaho-zastitnik-biskupije/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **29 May 1272 — The Statute of Dubrovnik**
  The city's law code, in eight books (the seventh is maritime law), framed Ragusan law until the Republic ended in 1808.
  - Anniversary 05-29 1272: The Statute of Dubrovnik was promulgated
  - Sources: [Liber Statutorum Ragusii (Hrčak)](https://hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/30920) — Scholarly & archival; [TZ Dubrovnik](https://tzdubrovnik.hr/lang/en/get/vijesti/80164/the_statute_of_dubrovnik_of_1272.html) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1278 — The notaries' books begin**
  The city hired a public notary, Tomasino de Savere. From here on contracts, wills and court business were written into registers that still survive in the State Archives in Dubrovnik — the oldest place to look for Ragusan families.
  - Sources: [JArchiveS: Diversa Notariae](https://arhiv.jas-center.eu/index.php/diversa-notariae-various-documents-of-the-public-notary;isad?sf_culture=hr) — Scholarly & archival; [JArchiveS: Testamenta Notariae](https://arhiv.jas-center.eu/index.php/testamenta-notariae-testamenta-de-notaria-wills-registered-at-the-public-notary) — Scholarly & archival

- [ ] **1333 — Ston and Pelješac bought**
  Ragusa bought Ston and the Pelješac peninsula from the Serbian king Stefan Dušan and the Bosnian ban Stjepan Kotromanić, then walled Ston to guard its salt pans.
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovačka Republika](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovacka-republika) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Dubrovačka Republika (hr)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrova%C4%8Dka_Republika) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1345 — Mljet**
  The island of Mljet came fully under Ragusa (the Great Council had first claimed it in 1301). The same year Mljet got its own statute, and its Benedictine abbey freed the islanders from their dues for a yearly 300 perpers.
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovačka Republika](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovacka-republika) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Proleksis enciklopedija: Mljet](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/37627/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Dubrovnik od XIII. do XV. stoljeća](https://dubrovnikportal.com/kolumne/crtice-iz-povijesti-grada/dubrovnik-od-xiii-do-xv-stoljeca/) — Travel guides & blogs

- [ ] **1358 — Free of Venice**
  By the Treaty of Zadar Venice gave up Dalmatia. At Visegrád Ragusa accepted the Hungarian-Croatian king as distant overlord — and from then on governed itself as a republic.
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovačka Republika](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovacka-republika) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Republic of Ragusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ragusa) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **27 July 1377 — The earliest known quarantine law**
  On 27 July 1377 the Great Council ordered that travellers from plague areas spend 30 days (the trentino) on the islet of Mrkan or in Cavtat before entering the city; the islets of Bobara and Supetar were used later too. The decree survives in the city’s law book, the Liber Viridis. The 40-day wait that later became the rule elsewhere, quaranta in Venetian, gave quarantine its name.
  - Anniversary 07-27 1377: Ragusa passed the earliest known quarantine law
  - Sources: [Quarantine, trade and health policies in Ragusa-Dubrovnik (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11624707/) — Scholarly & archival; [Lazzarettos of Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazzarettos_of_Dubrovnik) — Wikipedia & press; [Quarantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarantine) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1399 — The Littoral bought**
  King Ostoja of Bosnia sold Ragusa the coastland from Kuril to Ston, the Dubrovnik Littoral (Primorje), for 1,500 ducats, a house in the city and Ragusan citizenship. It was governed from Slano, whose first count took office in May 1399.
  - Sources: [Primorje (medieval župa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorje_(medieval_%C5%BEupa)) — Wikipedia & press; [Proleksis: Stjepan Ostoja](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/40117/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Dubrovačko primorje municipality: history](https://www.dubrovackoprimorje.hr/povijest) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **27 Jan 1416 — The slave trade banned**
  The council forbade citizens and foreigners alike to buy, sell or broker slaves in Ragusa or on its ships.
  - Anniversary 01-27 1416: Ragusa banned the slave trade
  - Sources: [Total Croatia News](https://total-croatia-news.com/news/politics/dubrovnik-marks-601-years-since-abolishing-slavery/) — Wikipedia & press; [Human Progress](https://humanprogress.org/centers-of-progress-pt-37-dubrovnik-public-health/) — Travel guides & blogs

- [ ] **1419–1426 — Konavle bought**
  The Republic bought Konavle in two halves, the east in 1419 and the west in 1426 — the story is told in the Konavle strand. Its land now ran along the coast to Cape Oštro, at the mouth of the Bay of Kotor.
  - Sources: [Konavle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konavle) — Wikipedia & press; [Proleksis: Konavle](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/32039/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Konavle](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/konavle) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1438–1444 — Water and bells**
  An aqueduct of about 12 km brought spring water from Šumet to Onofrio's fountains (1438–40); the bell tower on the Luža went up in 1444.
  - Sources: [Dubrovnik Bell Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrovnik_Bell_Tower) — Wikipedia & press; [Onofrio's Fountains](https://travelcurious.com/croatia/dubrovnik/attractions/onofrios-fountains-ARU5HJ) — Travel guides & blogs

- [ ] **1458 — Tribute to the Sultan — and freedom kept**
  Ragusa began paying the Sultan a yearly tribute, the harač: 1,500 gold ducats, first handed over in Skopje in 1458, in return for its freedom and its merchants’ rights across Ottoman lands. The same year the Ragusan merchant Benedetto Cotrugli (Benedikt Kotruljević) wrote the earliest known description of double-entry bookkeeping; it was printed only in 1573.
  - Sources: [Republic of Ragusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ragusa) — Wikipedia & press; [Benedetto Cotrugli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedetto_Cotrugli) — Wikipedia & press; [Vesna Miović: Dubrovnik in Ottoman documents (Hrčak)](https://hrcak.srce.hr/file/46061) — Scholarly & archival; [Turkish receipts for the Ragusan tribute (Hrčak)](https://hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/17872) — Scholarly & archival; [Hrvatski biografski leksikon: Kotruljević](https://hbl.lzmk.hr/clanak/250) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1460s — The walls rise**
  With the Ottomans at the door after 1453, Minčeta became a great round tower and Revelin was built to guard the land gate.
  - Sources: [Walls of Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Dubrovnik) — Wikipedia & press; [Dubrovnik City Walls (Society of Friends of Dubrovnik Antiquities)](https://citywallsdubrovnik.hr/western-outer-wall/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovnik](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovnik) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1551 — Dundo Maroje**
  Marin Držić’s comedy was first staged at Carnival in 1551, in the council hall beside the Rector’s Palace — the Golden Age of Ragusan letters.
  - Sources: [Marin Držić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_Dr%C5%BEi%C4%87) — Wikipedia & press; [Marin Držić lexicon: Dundo Maroje](https://leksikon.muzej-marindrzic.eu/dundo-maroje/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [House of Marin Držić: biography](https://muzej-marindrzic.eu/en/biography/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **6 April 1667 — The Great Earthquake**
  An earthquake and the fires after it all but razed the city; estimates of the dead run from about 3,000 to 5,000, the Rector among them. The cathedral and much of the Stradun were rebuilt afterwards, in the Baroque look you saw.
  - Anniversary 04-06 1667: The Great Earthquake struck Dubrovnik
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovnik](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovnik) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Potres u Dubrovniku 1667. (hr)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potres_u_Dubrovniku_1667.) — Wikipedia & press; [1667 Dubrovnik earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1667_Dubrovnik_earthquake) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1699 — Buffers against Venice**
  At the peace of Karlowitz, after Ragusan diplomats lobbied in Vienna and Constantinople, the Ottomans kept two narrow outlets to the sea, at Neum–Klek and at Sutorina, so that Venetian land would not touch the Republic’s — which is why Bosnia and Herzegovina still reaches the sea at Neum.
  - Sources: [Neum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neum) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Karlovački mir](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/karlovacki-mir) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovačka Republika](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovacka-republika) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **18 May 1711 — Ruđer Bošković born**
  The Ragusan Jesuit became one of Europe's great scientists — physicist, astronomer and founder of the Brera observatory in Milan. Dubrovnik's airport is named for him.
  - Anniversary 05-18 1711: Ruđer Bošković was born in Dubrovnik
  - Sources: [Ruđer Bošković Institute](https://irb.hr/eng/Education/Ruder-Josip-Boskovic) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1806 — The French arrive**
  On 27 May 1806 French troops under General Lauriston entered the city. From 17 June Russian and Montenegrin forces besieged it, burning the suburbs and the summer villas, until a French relief force under General Molitor drove them off on 6 July.
  - Anniversary 05-27 1806: French troops entered Dubrovnik
  - Sources: [Opsada Dubrovnika (1806.) (Croatian Wikipedia)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opsada_Dubrovnika_(1806.)) — Wikipedia & press; [Matica hrvatska: the war round Dubrovnik in 1806](https://www.matica.hr/kolo/310/ratovanje-oko-dubrovnika-godine-1806-20587/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Siege of Ragusa (1806)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ragusa_(1806)) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **31 Jan 1808 — The Republic ends**
  Marshal Marmont abolished the Republic of Ragusa. After Napoleon's fall the Congress of Vienna (1815) gave it to Austria, and it was never restored.
  - Anniversary 01-31 1808: Marmont abolished the Republic of Ragusa
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dubrovačka Republika](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dubrovacka-republika) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Walls of Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Dubrovnik) — Wikipedia & press; [Republic of Ragusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ragusa) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **18 Oct 1944 — The Partisans enter**
  German troops left and Yugoslav Partisans took the city on 18 October 1944. They arrested more than 300 citizens, and on 24–25 October executed 53 people without trial on the island of Daksa, whose remains were exhumed there in 2009.
  - Anniversary 10-18 1944: Yugoslav Partisans entered Dubrovnik
  - Sources: [Daksa massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daksa_massacre) — Wikipedia & press; [Daksa (island)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daksa_(island)) — Wikipedia & press; [HRT: the 53 victims of Daksa](https://vijesti.hrt.hr/hrvatska/odana-pocast-za-53-zrtve-ubijene-na-daksi-kod-dubrovnika-1944-12975503) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1979 — World Heritage**
  The Old City was added to UNESCO's World Heritage List.
  - Sources: [UNESCO: Old City of Dubrovnik](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/95/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1991–1992 — The siege**
  The Yugoslav navy declared a blockade of Croatia’s ports, Dubrovnik among them, on 17 September 1991, and the army besieged Dubrovnik from 1 October 1991 until its defenders broke out on 26 May 1992. The worst attack came on 6 December 1991; Fort Imperial on Srđ held.
  - Sources: [Opsada Dubrovnika (Croatian Wikipedia)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opsada_Dubrovnika) — Wikipedia & press; [Siege of Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Dubrovnik) — Wikipedia & press; [Museum of the Homeland War Dubrovnik](https://mdrd.hr/vro-oslobadanje-juga-hrvatske-i-deblokada-dubrovnika/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatski vojnik: the lifting of the siege](https://hrvatski-vojnik.hr/oslobadanje-juga-hrvatske-i-deblokada-dubrovnika/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **26 July 2022 — The Pelješac Bridge**
  The bridge joined the Dubrovnik area to the rest of Croatia by road without crossing Neum.
  - Anniversary 07-26 2022: The Pelješac Bridge opened
  - Sources: [Pelješac Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelje%C5%A1ac_Bridge) — Wikipedia & press; [Hina: the bridge opened](https://www.hina.hr/vijest/11058274) — Wikipedia & press; [Croatian Government: the opening](https://vlada.gov.hr/vijesti/otvorenje-peljeskog-mosta-velik-dogadjaj-za-hrvatsku/35836?lang=hr) — Encyclopedias & institutions

## Konavle

> The fertile valley south of Dubrovnik — older than the city itself, fought over by Serbian, Bosnian and Herzegovinian lords, then Ragusan farmland for almost 400 years.

- [ ] **Before Rome — Epidauros**
  Cavtat began as Epidauros, a settlement on the Illyrian coast. Some accounts call it a Greek colony; Croatian archaeologists note there isn't enough evidence to say so.
  - Sources: [Epigrafska baština rimskodobnog Epidaura (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/27220235_Epigrafska_bastina_rimskodobnog_Epidaura) — Scholarly & archival; [Epidaurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidaurum) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1st c. BC — Roman Epidaurum**
  Epidaurum was made a Roman colony, by most accounts in Caesar's time, as a reward for backing the winning side in Rome's civil war. Traces of its field grid, villas, an aqueduct near Vodovađa and a Mithras relief at Močići survive in Konavle.
  - Sources: [Epigrafska baština rimskodobnog Epidaura (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/27220235_Epigrafska_bastina_rimskodobnog_Epidaura) — Scholarly & archival; [Cavtat history (kompas.hr)](https://www.kompas.hr/cavtat/guide/history) — Travel guides & blogs

- [ ] **530s — A bishop of Epidaurum**
  Epidaurum had become a bishop's seat; its bishop took part in the Church councils held at Salona in the 530s.
  - Sources: [ToposText: Epidaurus](https://topostext.org/place/426182UEpi) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Akademija Art: Zapisi sa Cavtata](https://akademija-art.hr/2023/04/19/bozidar-prorocic-zapisi-sa-cavtata-i/) — Travel guides & blogs

- [ ] **c. 615 — Destroyed — and reborn as Ragusa** _(tradition)_
  Avars and Slavs destroyed Epidaurum; by tradition its people fled to found Ragusa. The town that grew again on the site was long called Ragusa Vecchia, 'Old Ragusa'.
  - Sources: [Epidaurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidaurum) — Wikipedia & press; [Cavtat history](https://www.kompas.hr/cavtat/guide/history) — Travel guides & blogs

- [ ] **c. 950 — Konavli in Travunia**
  Constantine VII's De Administrando Imperio has a chapter "Of the Terbounians and Kanalites": the Kanalites were the Slavs of Konavle, ruled together with Travunia between Ragusa and the Bay of Kotor.
  - Sources: [Kanalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanalites) — Wikipedia & press; [Proleksis enciklopedija: Konavle](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/32039/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1166–1196 — Under the Nemanjić**
  Stefan Nemanja brought Travunia, and with it Konavle, under Serbian rule. It stayed Serbian until the Nemanjić line ended in 1371, then passed through the hands of local lords.
  - Sources: [Stefan Nemanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Nemanja) — Wikipedia & press; [Nemanjić dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemanji%C4%87_dynasty) — Wikipedia & press; [Proleksis: Konavle](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/32039/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Nemanjići](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/nemanjici) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1373–1378 — Lords come and go**
  After the Serbian empire broke up, Konavle passed to Nikola Altomanović, then the Balšić lords of Zeta. In 1377–78 King Tvrtko I took it for Bosnia, and the Sanković family held it for him. Sokol Grad, the fortress above Dunave, first appears in records in 1373.
  - Sources: [Proleksis enciklopedija: Konavle](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/32039/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Konavle (područje), bs](https://bs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konavle_(podru%C4%8Dje)) — Wikipedia & press; [Sokol (Konavle)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokol_(Konavle)) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1391 — A sale blocked**
  On 15 April 1391 the Sanković brothers sold Konavle to Ragusa, without the Bosnian king’s consent. Before Ragusa could take it, the Bosnian lords Vlatko Vuković and Pavle Radinović seized Radič Sanković and split Konavle between themselves.
  - Sources: [Radič Sanković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radi%C4%8D_Sankovi%C4%87) — Wikipedia & press; [Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina) — Wikipedia & press; [Paula Kaulić: Ragusa’s expansion into Konavle (Hrčak)](https://hrcak.srce.hr/file/250172) — Scholarly & archival; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Vlatko Vuković Kosača](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/kosaca-vlatko-vukovic) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1419 — Upper Konavle bought**
  Sandalj Hranić sold the eastern half to Ragusa for 12,000 ducats and a yearly 500 perpers.
  - Sources: [Proleksis enciklopedija: Konavle](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/32039/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Radoslav Pavlović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radoslav_Pavlovi%C4%87_Radinovi%C4%87) — Wikipedia & press; [Konavle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konavle) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1426 — Lower Konavle and Cavtat bought**
  Radoslav Pavlović sold the western half, with Cavtat and Obod, for 18,000 perpers (the deed is dated 31 December 1426). From 1427 a Ragusan rector governed Konavle from his palace at Pridvorje until 1808.
  - Anniversary 12-31 1426: Ragusa bought Lower Konavle and Cavtat
  - Sources: [Proleksis enciklopedija: Konavle](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/32039/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Radoslav Pavlović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radoslav_Pavlovi%C4%87_Radinovi%C4%87) — Wikipedia & press; [Konavle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konavle) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1430–1433 — The Konavle War**
  Radoslav Pavlović tried to take back what he had sold. Peace was made on 25 October 1432 and confirmed by the Bosnian king in March 1433, and Konavle stayed Ragusan.
  - Sources: [Radoslav Pavlović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radoslav_Pavlovi%C4%87_Radinovi%C4%87) — Wikipedia & press; [Pavlovića Zemlja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlovi%C4%87a_Zemlja) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Pavlovići](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/pavlovici) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatski biografski leksikon: Kosače](https://hbl.lzmk.hr/clanak/kosace) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [The Bosnian king in the Konavle War, 1430–1433 (Hrčak)](https://hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/118299) — Scholarly & archival

- [ ] **1451–1454 — Herceg Stjepan's war**
  Stjepan Vukčić Kosača, Sandalj’s nephew, went to war with Ragusa over trade and over Konavle, and his troops overran it. Peace came on 10 April 1454 with nothing settled, and Konavle stayed Ragusan.
  - Sources: [Stjepan Vukčić Kosača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stjepan_Vuk%C4%8Di%C4%87_Kosa%C4%8Da) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Stjepan Vukčić Kosača](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/kosaca-stjepan-vukcic) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatski biografski leksikon: Kosače](https://hbl.lzmk.hr/clanak/kosace) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1420s–1672 — Sokol Grad on guard**
  Sokol Grad, the fortress above Dunave, came to the Republic with Konavle and was kept with a garrison under a Ragusan castellan. It no longer appears as a military post in the records of 1672, and was probably abandoned around then.
  - Sources: [Sokol (Konavle)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokol_(Konavle)) — Wikipedia & press; [Total Croatia: the Republic’s highest fortress](https://total-croatia-news.com/lifestyle/fortress-dubrovnik-republic/) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1799–1800 — The Konavle Revolt**
  Rising dues, labour on noble estates and a 10 May 1799 order forcing everyone over 15 to buy state salt drove more than 800 Konavle householders to resist ("Nećete pro Zvekovice"). The revolt failed, but showed how weak the Republic had become.
  - Anniversary 05-10 1799: The salt order that sparked the Konavle Revolt
  - Sources: [Konavoska buna (Konavle museums)](https://blog.migk.hr/2020/05/10/konavoska-buna-10-svibnja-1799/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [DubrovnikNet](https://www.dubrovniknet.hr/niko-kapetanic-konavoska-buna-ili-necete-pro-zvekovice-1/) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1806 — War reaches Cavtat**
  Russian and Montenegrin forces crossed into Konavle. On 30 May about a hundred French soldiers held off some 300 Montenegrins near Zvekovica for hours, but on 3 June the French left Cavtat, and its people went with them. Cavtat was looted, and around 235 houses in Konavle — about a quarter of them — were burned.
  - Anniversary 05-30 1806: French troops held off Montenegrin forces near Zvekovica
  - Anniversary 06-03 1806: The French left Cavtat
  - Sources: [Matica hrvatska (Kolo): Ratovanje oko Dubrovnika 1806.](https://www.matica.hr/kolo/310/ratovanje-oko-dubrovnika-godine-1806-20587/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Opsada Dubrovnika (1806.) (hr)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opsada_Dubrovnika_(1806.)) — Wikipedia & press; [Konavle museums: Ukidanje Dubrovačke Republike](https://blog.migk.hr/2026/01/12/ukidanje-dubrovacke-republike/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1834 · 1855 — Two sons of Cavtat**
  The jurist Baltazar Bogišić (1834–1908) and the painter Vlaho Bukovac (1855–1922) were both born in Cavtat; Bukovac's birth house is now a museum.
  - Sources: [Time Out: Cavtat](https://timeout.com/croatia/things-to-do/the-best-things-to-do-in-cavtat) — Travel guides & blogs; [Konavle museums blog](https://blog.migk.hr/en/2023/08/16/the-role-of-vlaho-bukovac-in-the-creation-of-a-monument-to-baltazar-bogisic/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1936 · 1962 — From Gruda to Čilipi**
  Flights began at an airfield at Gruda in 1936; the present airport at Čilipi opened in 1962.
  - Sources: [Dubrovnik Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrovnik_Airport) — Wikipedia & press; [The Aviation Hub](https://theaviationhub.co.uk/dubrovnik-airport-guide/) — Travel guides & blogs

- [ ] **1941–1943 — Konavle cut in two**
  In 1941 western Konavle, run from Cavtat, went to the Axis Independent State of Croatia; the east around Gruda was annexed by Italy, which took Kuna and Pridvorje too in 1942. When Italy surrendered, German troops entered Cavtat on 9 September 1943.
  - Anniversary 09-09 1943: German troops entered Cavtat
  - Sources: [Konavle u Drugom svjetskom ratu (Konavle museums)](https://blog.migk.hr/2024/05/07/konavle-u-drugom-svjetskom-ratu/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Prevlaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevlaka) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **Oct 1991 — Occupied**
  The first shells fell on 22 September 1991, on Đurinići and Vitaljina in eastern Konavle. Yugoslav army units burned Čilipi between 5 and 7 October 1991 (an indictment lists 90 family houses destroyed) and took Cavtat on 15 October. Konavle stayed occupied for a year, and many of its people were held at Morinj in Montenegro; its villages were rebuilt over the years that followed.
  - Sources: [Dubrovnik Insider](https://dubrovnikinsider.hr/krajem-rujna-1991-neprijatelj-je-poceo-pucati-po-konavlima/) — Wikipedia & press; [tportal (indictment)](https://www.tportal.hr/vijesti/clanak/palio-cilipe-stize-ga-pravda-20121206) — Wikipedia & press; [Morinj camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morinj_camp) — Wikipedia & press; [Opsada Dubrovnika (Croatian Wikipedia)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opsada_Dubrovnika) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1992 — Freed**
  Under the Tuđman–Ćosić agreement of 30 September 1992 the Yugoslav army withdrew, and Operation Konavle, from the night of 20–21 October 1992, freed the last occupied part of the Dubrovnik area.
  - Sources: [Operacija Konavle (Croatian Wikipedia)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operacija_Konavle) — Wikipedia & press; [narod.hr](https://narod.hr/kultura/21-listopada-1992-operacija-konavle-osloboden-posljednji-dio-okupiranog-dubrovackog-podrucja-i-juzne-hrvatske) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **2026 — Epidaurum still giving up secrets**
  Konavle's museum archaeologists found a sealed, untouched late Roman stone sarcophagus (4th–6th century) in Epidaurum's cemetery in Cavtat.

## Dalmatia

> The long coast Ragusa belonged to and stood apart from: Illyrian, Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Croatian, Hungarian, Venetian, Austrian — and Croatian again.

- [ ] **4th c. BC — Illyrians and Greeks**
  The coast is Illyrian land (the Delmatae tribe gave Dalmatia its name), with Greek colonies on the islands: Issa on Vis, founded by Dionysius of Syracuse in the 390s BC, and Pharos on Hvar, from about 385 BC.
  - Sources: [History of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dalmatia) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Greek colonisation of the Adriatic](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/natuknica.aspx?id=23213) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Delmati](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/delmati) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **229 BC — Rome arrives**
  Rome fought the Illyrian queen Teuta and began two centuries of conquest. It took Salona in 78 BC.
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dalmacija](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/13743) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Salona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salona) — Wikipedia & press; [History of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dalmatia) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **6–9 AD — The Great Illyrian Revolt**
  After crushing the revolt Rome organised the province of Dalmatia, with its capital at Salona near Split.
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dalmacija](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/13743) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [History of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dalmatia) — Wikipedia & press; [Salona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salona) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **295–305 — Diocletian's palace**
  The Dalmatian-born emperor built his retirement palace at Spalatum — the heart of today's Split.
  - Sources: [Leksikografski zavod (HEMU): Dalmacija](https://hemu.lzmk.hr/natuknica.aspx?ID=10815) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [History of Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Split) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **480 — The last Western emperor**
  Julius Nepos, driven out of Italy in 475, went on ruling from Dalmatia, still recognised in Constantinople as Western emperor, until he was murdered in Diocletian’s palace on 9 May 480. The Ostrogoths took the province in 493; Justinian’s armies won it back from 535.
  - Sources: [Salona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salona) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Julije Nepot](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/nepot-julije) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Britannica: Julius Nepos](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Julius-Nepos) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **7th c. — Salona falls, Split is born**
  Avar and Slav invasions ended Roman Salona; its people moved into Diocletian's palace. Byzantium kept only the islands and a few coast towns — Zadar, Split, Trogir and Ragusa among them.
  - Sources: [History of Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Split) — Wikipedia & press; [Dalmatia (theme)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia_(theme)) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Split](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/split) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Diocletian’s Palace](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dioklecijanova-palaca) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **c. 870 — The Theme of Dalmatia**
  Byzantium governed its Dalmatian towns as a province ruled from Zadar, until about 1060.
  - Sources: [Dalmatia (theme)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia_(theme)) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: tema](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/natuknica.aspx?id=60765) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Proleksis: Dalmacija](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/16749/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **925 · 1060s — Kings of Croatia and Dalmatia**
  Pope John X’s letter of 925 calls Tomislav king of the Croats. Peter Krešimir IV is the first ruler recorded as king of Croatia and Dalmatia, and from about 1069 the Dalmatian towns came under his crown.
  - Sources: [Kingdom of Croatia (925–1102)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Croatia_(925%E2%80%931102)) — Wikipedia & press; [Peter Krešimir IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kre%C5%A1imir_IV) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Tomislav](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/tomislav) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Petar Krešimir IV](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/petar-kresimir-iv) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1102 — Union with Hungary** _(debated)_
  Croatia and Dalmatia came under the Hungarian king. The famous Pacta conventa describing the deal is now thought to be a later copy or forgery.
  - Sources: [Pacta conventa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacta_conventa_(Croatia)) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Pacta conventa](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/natuknica.aspx?id=46073) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Proleksis: Koloman](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/31853/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1358 — Venice out**
  The Treaty of Zadar made Venice give up Dalmatia to Louis I of Hungary — the same deal that freed Ragusa.
  - Sources: [Proleksis enciklopedija: Dalmacija](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/16749/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Republic of Ragusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ragusa) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1409–1420 — Sold to Venice**
  Ladislaus of Naples sold his claim to Dalmatia to Venice for 100,000 ducats; by 1420 Venice ruled the coast, which it kept until 1797. Ragusa stayed outside.
  - Anniversary 07-09 1409: Ladislaus of Naples sold his claim to Dalmatia to Venice
  - Sources: [Proleksis enciklopedija: Dalmacija](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/16749/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Prodaja Dalmacije Mlecima 1409. (hr)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodaja_Dalmacije_Mlecima_1409.) — Wikipedia & press; [Dalmatia under Venetian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia_under_Venetian_rule) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1797–1815 — Venice falls**
  Austria took Venetian Dalmatia in 1797; Napoleon's France had it from 1805/06 and folded it, with Ragusa, into the Illyrian Provinces; the Congress of Vienna returned it all to Austria.
  - Sources: [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dalmacija](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/13743) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Dalmatia under Venetian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia_under_Venetian_rule) — Wikipedia & press; [Kingdom of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dalmatia) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1815–1918 — The Kingdom of Dalmatia**
  An Austrian crown land, then part of Austria-Hungary, with its capital at Zadar — Dubrovnik and Konavle included.
  - Sources: [Leksikografski zavod (HEMU): Dalmacija](https://hemu.lzmk.hr/natuknica.aspx?ID=10815) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Kingdom of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dalmatia) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1918 — Into Yugoslavia**
  Dalmatia joined the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (Yugoslavia from 1929). Under the Treaty of Rapallo (1920) Zadar and the island of Lastovo went to Italy, which kept them until 1947.
  - Sources: [Treaty of Rapallo (1920)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Rapallo_(1920)) — Wikipedia & press; [Britannica: Dalmatia](https://www.britannica.com/place/Dalmatia) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [History of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dalmatia) — Wikipedia & press

- [ ] **1941–1943 — Divided by the Axis**
  The Treaties of Rome (18 May 1941) gave Italy much of the coast as the Governorate of Dalmatia — near Dubrovnik, the islands of Korčula and Mljet — while Šipan, Lopud and Lokrum stayed in the Independent State of Croatia. Italy's surrender in September 1943 ended the arrangement.
  - Anniversary 05-18 1941: The Treaties of Rome divided Dalmatia
  - Sources: [Rimski ugovori (1941.) (hr)](https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimski_ugovori_(1941.)) — Wikipedia & press; [Treaties of Rome (1941)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_Rome_(1941)) — Wikipedia & press; [Governorate of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_of_Dalmatia) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Rimski ugovori](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/rimski-ugovori) — Encyclopedias & institutions

- [ ] **1991 — Croatia**
  Dalmatia became part of independent Croatia, at the price of the Homeland War of 1991–95.
  - Sources: [History of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dalmatia) — Wikipedia & press; [Hrvatska enciklopedija: Dalmacija](https://www.enciklopedija.hr/clanak/dalmacija) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Proleksis: Domovinski rat](https://proleksis.lzmk.hr/18243/) — Encyclopedias & institutions

## Finding the family in the records

- [ ] Church birth (baptism) registers only became compulsory with the Council of Trent’s decree of 11 November 1563. Croatia’s oldest parish register is Umag’s in Istria, from 1483, though its earliest entries survive as copies made after 1608; in the Dubrovnik area the oldest is a 1580 death register from Suđurađ on Šipan.
- [ ] For the 1400s, Ragusa's own government records are where families appear: the notarial and chancery books (Diversa Notariae, Diversa Cancellariae) and registered wills (Testamenta Notariae), kept from 1278–1282 onward in the State Archives in Dubrovnik, plus confraternity rolls such as the Antunini register.
- [ ] Konavle became Ragusan in 1419–1426, so from then on Konavle families turn up in the Republic's land, tax and court records. Konavle's surviving parish registers mostly begin in the late 1700s (Veljaci's baptisms run from 1786).
- [ ] Parish registers taken after 1945 were returned to the Dubrovnik diocese in 2022; FamilySearch also holds digitised Croatian church books.
  - Sources: [Umag baptismal register 1483–1643 (State Archives in Pazin)](https://www.dapa.hr/predstavljena-je-knjiga-maticna-knjiga-krstenih-zupe-umag-1483-1643/) — Scholarly & archival; [Istrian encyclopedia: parish registers](https://istra.lzmk.hr/clanak/maticne-knjige) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Dubrovačka biskupija (registers returned)](https://db.hr/dubrovackoj-biskupiji-vraceno-210-maticnih-knjige-oduzetih-nakon-drugog-svjetskog-rata/) — Encyclopedias & institutions; [Croatian church records (exyugenealogy)](https://exyugenealogy.net/archives-church-records/) — Travel guides & blogs; [Testamenta Notariae (JArchiveS)](https://arhiv.jas-center.eu/index.php/testamenta-notariae-testamenta-de-notaria-wills-registered-at-the-public-notary) — Scholarly & archival; [Antunini of Dubrovnik (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/267897109_U_predvorju_vlasti_Dubrovacki_antunini_u_kasnom_srednjem_vijeku_On_the_Threshold_of_Power_The_Antunini_of_Dubrovnik_in_the_Late_Middle_Ages) — Scholarly & archival; [Veljaci baptisms 1786–1808](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318396344_Analiza_maticne_knjige_krstenih_danasnje_zupe_Veljaci_1786_-_1808) — Scholarly & archival; [FamilySearch: Croatia church books](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Croatia,_Church_Books_-_FamilySearch_Historical_Records) — Travel guides & blogs

## The siege on the map

- [ ] **Dubrovnik Airport, Čilipi** — Looted in October 1991, its equipment partly carried off to Montenegro, while army units burned Čilipi village.
- [ ] **Lapad, where you stayed** — In November 1991 shells fell on Lapad and Babin Kuk, whose hotels were full of refugees from Konavle, Župa and the Littoral.
- [ ] **The port of Gruž** — Where the Libertas convoy came in through the blockade, and where the ferry Slavija took almost 4,000 people out on 14 November 1991.
- [ ] **Cavtat** — Taken on 15 October 1991. Konavle stayed occupied for a year, until Operation Konavle landed here on the night of 20–21 October 1992.
- [ ] **Stradun and the Old Town** — Shelled for more than ten hours on 6 December 1991; a plaque under the bell tower remembers the photographer Pavo Urban, killed there that day. In all, 563 of the Old Town’s 824 buildings were hit in 1991–92, by the survey the Hague tribunal cited.
- [ ] **Fort Imperial on Srđ** — On 6 December 1991 a few dozen defenders held it against infantry and tanks; had it fallen, the city lay open below. It now houses the Museum of the Homeland War.
- [ ] **Mount Srđ** — The front line ran across Srđ: the defenders held Fort Imperial while the army held Bosanka just behind it. The summit cross, pulled down in 1991, was raised again in October 1997.
- [ ] **The cable car** — Wrecked in the 1991 fighting; it has carried people up Srđ again since July 2010.
- [ ] **Konavle** (occupied area) — Cavtat was taken on 15 October 1991, and the whole valley stayed occupied until Operation Konavle, 20–24 October 1992.
- [ ] **Župa dubrovačka** (occupied area) — Occupied in late October 1991, when Kupari and Srebreno fell; freed when Dubrovnik’s defenders broke out on 26 May 1992.
- [ ] **The Dubrovnik Littoral** (occupied area) — Slano fell on 4–5 October 1991. The Littoral was freed in May and June 1992.
- [ ] **Rijeka dubrovačka and the coast to Brsečine** (occupied area) — Taken in October and November 1991: Bosanka on the Srđ plateau fell on 9 November, Mokošica in late November after Osojnik. Freed from 26 May 1992.
- [ ] **Fort Imperial** — On 6 December 1991 a few dozen defenders held the fort against an infantry and tank attack from Bosanka and Strinčjera, with tanks right up at the fort. It was the anchor of the line that saved the city, and today it houses the Museum of the Homeland War.

## The History map, era by era

- [ ] **500 BC — The Greek World & Persia** — Greek city-states led by Athens and Sparta face the Persian Empire across the Aegean. Illyrian and Thracian tribes hold the interior.
  - Ragusa: _Not yet founded_ — The coast where Cavtat stands is Illyrian land; Greek traders sail these waters.
- [ ] **323 BC — Alexander the Great** — Macedon conquers Greece and the Persian Empire. Alexander dies in Babylon in 323 BC.
  - Ragusa: _Not yet founded_ — The southern Adriatic coast is Illyrian territory, outside Alexander's empire.
- [ ] **300 BC — The Successor Kingdoms** — Alexander's generals carve up his empire: Kassander takes Macedon, Lysimachus takes Thrace.
  - Ragusa: _Not yet founded_ — Greek traders sail the Adriatic; the Illyrian kingdoms control this coast.
- [ ] **200 BC — Rome Arrives** — Rome crosses the Adriatic. Macedon and its Hellenic League will fall to Rome by 168 BC.
  - Ragusa: _Not yet founded_ — Rome fights the Illyrian queen Teuta's kingdom (229 BC) and starts to control the coast.
- [ ] **100 BC — Roman Republic** — Macedonia and Greece are Roman provinces. The Dacians hold the lands north of the Danube.
  - Ragusa: _Not yet founded_ — The coast is now part of Roman Illyricum.
- [ ] **14 AD — Augustus: The Roman Empire** — Rome rules almost the whole peninsula. Dacia remains independent beyond the Danube.
  - Ragusa: _Roman Epidaurum_ — Epidaurum (Cavtat) is a Roman colony — the town whose refugees will later found Ragusa.
- [ ] **200 AD — The Roman Empire at its Height** — Trajan conquers Dacia (106 AD). The Danube frontier is lined with legions and new cities.
  - Ragusa: _Roman Epidaurum_ — Province of Dalmatia. Epidaurum prospers on the coastal road to Greece.
- [ ] **300 AD — The Tetrarchy** — Diocletian splits rule among four emperors. Balkan cities like Sirmium and Thessalonica become imperial seats.
  - Ragusa: _Roman Epidaurum_ — Dalmatia is Diocletian's home province — he retires to his palace at Split.
- [ ] **400 AD — East and West Divide** — The empire splits for good in 395. The border runs through the Balkans — roughly today's Bosnia–Serbia line.
  - Ragusa: _Roman Epidaurum_ — The East–West dividing line runs just inland of this coast.
- [ ] **500 AD — Ostrogoths & Byzantium** — The West has fallen. Theodoric's Ostrogoths rule Italy and Dalmatia; Constantinople rules the rest.
  - Ragusa: _Roman Epidaurum_ — Dalmatia passes to the Ostrogoths, then back to Byzantium (530s).
- [ ] **650 AD — Avars & Slavs** — Avars and Slavs overrun the interior. By tradition, refugees from Epidaurum found Ragusa on a rocky islet (c. 614).
  - Ragusa: _Founded c. 614_ — By tradition, the people of Epidaurum, fleeing Avar and Slav raids, settle the islet of Laus: Ragusa. Byzantine protection.
- [ ] **700 AD — The Bulgars Arrive** — The First Bulgarian Empire is founded south of the Danube in 681, with its capital at Pliska.
  - Ragusa: _Byzantine town_ — A small walled town under Byzantine protection; Slavic Dubrava grows on the mainland opposite.
- [ ] **900 AD — Medieval Kingdoms Emerge** — Croatia, Serbia and Bulgaria take shape (Croatia becomes a kingdom c. 925). Bulgaria is Christian; Magyars settle the Danube basin.
  - Ragusa: _Byzantine city_ — Ragusa resists an Arab siege (866–67) and becomes a self-governing Byzantine city.
- [ ] **1000 — Byzantium Strikes Back** — Emperor Basil II wages war on Tsar Samuel of Bulgaria; by 1018 Bulgaria is absorbed. Hungary becomes a kingdom.
  - Ragusa: _Byzantine city_ — Self-governing under Byzantium, trading across the Adriatic.
- [ ] **1100 — Byzantium & Hungary** — Byzantium rules the south; Croatia enters a union with the Hungarian crown (1102).
  - Ragusa: _Byzantine city_ — By tradition, the channel between the islet and the mainland town is filled in and becomes the Stradun.
- [ ] **1210 — The Fourth Crusade** — Crusaders sack Constantinople (1204) and carve up Byzantium with Venice. Greek successor states rule from Nicaea and Arta.
  - Ragusa: _Under Venice_ — 1205: after the Fourth Crusade, Venice takes sovereignty — but Ragusa keeps its own laws, council and trade.
- [ ] **1300 — Restored Byzantium, Rising Serbia** — Byzantium retakes Constantinople (1261) but is weakened. Serbia under the Nemanjić dynasty expands south.
  - Ragusa: _Under Venice_ — Lastovo joins (mid-1200s). Ragusan merchants work the Serbian and Bosnian silver mines.
- [ ] **1355 — The Serbian Empire of Dušan** — Stefan Dušan, crowned Emperor in Skopje (1346), rules from the Danube to the Gulf of Corinth. The Ottomans land at Gallipoli (1354).
  - Ragusa: _Under Venice_ — Ston and Pelješac bought from Serbia and Bosnia (1333), Mljet added (1345). In 1358 Venice gives up Dalmatia; Ragusa accepts the distant overlordship of the Hungarian–Croatian king and governs itself.
- [ ] **1453 — Fall of Constantinople** — The Ottomans take Constantinople and make it their capital. The last Serbian, Bosnian, Albanian and Greek states fall within 30 years.
  - Ragusa: _Free Republic_ — The Littoral (Primorje) added (1399), Konavle (1419–26). From 1458 it pays tribute to the Sultan — and keeps its freedom.
- [ ] **1526 — Mohács** — Suleiman the Magnificent takes Belgrade (1521) and destroys the Hungarian army at Mohács (1526). Buda falls in 1541.
  - Ragusa: _Free Republic_ — A tributary of the Sultan but self-governing; Ragusan merchants hold trading privileges across the Ottoman Balkans.
- [ ] **1600 — The Ottoman Balkans** — After Mohács (1526) Hungary is partitioned. The Habsburgs hold a thin frontier; Venice keeps the coast; Ragusa pays tribute and trades.
  - Ragusa: _Golden Age_ — A neutral merchant republic trading between the Ottomans and Christian Europe. Motto: LIBERTAS.
- [ ] **1700 — Treaty of Karlowitz** — After the failed siege of Vienna (1683) the Habsburgs take Hungary and Slavonia; Venice takes the Morea. At Ragusa’s urging the Ottomans keep a corridor to the sea at Neum.
  - Ragusa: _Republic_ — 1667 earthquake devastates the city. 1699: at Ragusa’s urging, the Ottomans keep corridors to the sea at Neum and Sutorina, so that Venice’s new lands do not touch the Republic.
- [ ] **1800 — The End of Venice** — Napoleon ends the Venetian Republic (1797). Austria takes Dalmatia; the Ionian Islands become a Russo-Ottoman protectorate.
  - Ragusa: _Republic_ — Venice is gone (1797); Ragusa still stands — but Napoleon's armies are near.
- [ ] **1810 — Napoleon's Illyrian Provinces** — French troops end the Republic of Ragusa (1808). The coast from Ljubljana to Kotor becomes part of the French Empire. Serbs revolt (1804).
  - Ragusa: _Abolished 1808_ — Marshal Marmont dissolves the Republic after ~450 years. It joins the Illyrian Provinces.
- [ ] **1832 — Greek & Serbian Independence** — Greece wins independence (1830–32). Serbia is an autonomous principality. Ragusa is now Austrian Dalmatia.
  - Ragusa: _Austrian rule_ — 1815 Congress of Vienna: the Republic is not restored. Part of Austria's Kingdom of Dalmatia.
- [ ] **Mar 1878 — Treaty of San Stefano** — Russia defeats the Ottomans and dictates a huge Bulgaria from the Danube to the Aegean. Britain and Austria object.
  - Ragusa: _Austrian rule_ — A provincial Austrian town; the Republic is a memory. The Neum strip is still Ottoman Bosnia.
- [ ] **Jul 1878 — Congress of Berlin** — The powers cut "Big Bulgaria" back: a small principality, plus autonomous Eastern Rumelia. Serbia, Montenegro, Romania win independence.
  - Ragusa: _Austrian rule_ — Austria-Hungary occupies Bosnia; the Neum strip, though, stays Bosnian.
- [ ] **1913 — The Balkan Wars** — The Ottomans lose almost all of Europe; they keep Edirne. Albania is born. In 1914 a shot in Sarajevo starts the First World War.
  - Ragusa: _Austrian rule_ — A quiet Habsburg port on the eve of war.
- [ ] **1920 — After the First World War** — Austria-Hungary collapses. The Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (Yugoslavia) unites the South Slavs. Romania doubles.
  - Ragusa: _Yugoslavia_ — 1918: Dubrovnik joins the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats & Slovenes.
- [ ] **1938 — The Interwar Balkans** — Republican Turkey rules from Ankara (since 1923); kings rule Yugoslavia, Romania, Bulgaria, Greece and Albania.
  - Ragusa: _Yugoslavia_ — Part of the Banovina of Croatia (1939).
- [ ] **1942 — Axis Occupation** — Germany and its allies invade in 1941. Yugoslavia is carved up and Greece occupied; resistance and civil war last until 1945.
  - Ragusa: _Axis occupation_ — Part of the Axis puppet Independent State of Croatia, garrisoned by Italy. Partisans take the city in October 1944.
- [ ] **1960 — The Cold War** — Tito's socialist Yugoslavia stays non-aligned. Albania, Bulgaria and Romania are communist; Greece and Turkey join NATO.
  - Ragusa: _Yugoslavia_ — Tourism booms. UNESCO World Heritage listing follows in 1979.
- [ ] **1992 — War and Independence** — Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Macedonia declare independence (1991–92). Croatia defends itself in the Homeland War, and war engulfs Bosnia and Herzegovina; Serbia and Montenegro form FR Yugoslavia.
  - Ragusa: _Under siege_ — Oct 1991 – May 1992: the Yugoslav army besieges Dubrovnik; Konavle and Cavtat are occupied and the Old Town is shelled.
- [ ] **1995 — Dayton** — Croatia frees most of its occupied land in Operation Storm (Aug 1995); the Dayton Agreement (Nov 1995) ends the war in Bosnia and Herzegovina and divides it into two entities.
  - Ragusa: _Croatia_ — The Old Town is on UNESCO's "in danger" list (1991–98) while its shell damage is repaired.
- [ ] **2026 — The Balkans Today** — Montenegro (2006) and Kosovo (2008, recognition disputed) complete the map. Dubrovnik is in Croatia.
  - Ragusa: _Croatia_ — The 2022 Pelješac Bridge links the Dubrovnik area to the rest of Croatia, bypassing Neum.

## Glossary

- [ ] **De Administrando Imperio** — A handbook on the empire’s neighbours written for the Byzantine emperor Constantine VII around 950 — the earliest written account of Ragusa’s founding.
- [ ] **Theme of Dalmatia** — A Byzantine province of the Dalmatian coastal towns (about 870–1060), governed from Zadar.
- [ ] **Independent State of Croatia** — The NDH: an Axis puppet state set up in 1941 under Ante Pavelić, which lasted until 1945.
- [ ] **Great Council** — The assembly of all adult male nobles of Ragusa. It elected the officials and voted on the laws the Senate proposed.
- [ ] **Kanalites** — The Slavs of Konavle (“Kanali”), as Constantine VII’s handbook calls them.
- [ ] **Travunia** — A medieval Slav principality around Trebinje, inland from Dubrovnik. Konavle belonged to it.
- [ ] **perpers** — Ragusa’s own unit of money (the perpera), used for prices, tribute and fines.
- [ ] **ducats** — Gold coins, first struck in Venice in 1284, that were the trading money of the Mediterranean.
- [ ] **castellan** — The commander of a castle, such as the Republic’s fortress at Sokol Grad in Konavle.
- [ ] **rector** — The Republic’s head of state. He presided over the councils but had little power of his own; Konavle had its own rector at Pridvorje.
- [ ] **Laus** — The rocky islet where, by tradition, the first Ragusa was built; the city’s name is linked to it.
- [ ] **ban** — A ruler’s title in Bosnia and Croatia, roughly “lord” or “viceroy”.
